Join and Merge Audio Files

Combine multiple supported audio files into one continuous output file directly in your browser. This is useful for joining song sections, voice recordings, podcast segments, sound effects or other clips that need to play in a specific order. Add the files, arrange them in the sequence you want and export the combined result.

Before merging, listen to the source clips and check that their beginnings and endings are clean. Large differences in volume between clips will remain noticeable after joining, so it can help to adjust levels beforehand when necessary. The joiner focuses on combining files; it does not automatically master, normalize or remix the material. Browser and device codec support can affect which source formats can be decoded.

Processing is handled locally in the browser rather than by uploading the audio to a remote processing server. If a clip needs trimming before it is joined, use the Audio Cutter first. For smoother transitions at the boundaries, the Audio Fade tool can add fade-ins or fade-outs.

Combining clips in a controlled order

Combine multiple supported audio files into one sequence and control their order before export. Joining is useful for interviews, voice notes, song sections, intros or other clips that need to become a single continuous file.

When this tool is useful

An audio joiner can assemble interviews, voice notes, song parts, intros or several exported segments into one continuous file. Check the order before processing and listen at every join, especially when clips were recorded at different levels.

Practical limits and quality checks

If one clip is much louder than another, normalising or adjusting volume first can produce a smoother result. Joining files does not automatically fix differences in noise, sample rate, channel layout or mastering, so complex projects may still benefit from a dedicated audio editor.

Privacy and responsible use

Files selected for joining are processed in your browser. Only combine recordings you are entitled to use, keep the originals, and listen across every join point before relying on the merged output.

Frequently asked questions

Can browser audio processing change sound quality?

Joining clips with matching technical properties can be straightforward, but browser export may still re-encode the result. Different sample rates, channels or codecs can also require conversion, so use a lossless output if the merged file will be edited again.

Why can results differ between browsers or devices?

Large projects consume memory because several audio files may be decoded at once. For reliable results, use files with compatible formats where possible, close other demanding tabs and split exceptionally long assemblies into smaller stages.
